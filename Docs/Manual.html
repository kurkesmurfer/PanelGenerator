PanelGenerator Manual

Designing VCV Rack and MetaModule front panels, and generating the artwork and C++ that a module compiles against, from one document.

At a glance

The .panelgen document is the only source. You draw the panel, give each control a role and an identifier, and emit: PanelGenerator writes the panel SVG, the artwork for any custom widgets, and a C++ header that declares the parameter, input, output and light enums and places every control. Artwork and headers are build output: regenerate them, never edit them. --compare then proves that the code you build still matches the document.

Around that loop the editor offers house styles (design languages: Kurkesmurfer, Serge, LCARS, Paperface) with their own palettes, grids and title rules; precise placement (grids with half positions, smart guides, label slots, numeric centre coordinates); a pen and shape operations; light and dark themes; and importers that read an existing panel SVG or a module's C++.

  • 1 HP = 15 px = 5.08 mm
  • 3U = 380 px = 128.5 mm
  • panel pixels are 75 dpi
  • mm → px ×2.953
  • SVG exported with sizes in mm

1 · Install and run

PanelGenerator needs macOS 13 or later and the Xcode command line tools.

make app        # build and assemble PanelGenerator.app, then: open PanelGenerator.app
make run        # build debug and launch
make test       # unit tests
make selftest   # headless render test → Docs/pg_demo.{svg,png}

The same binary is also the command-line tool (§14): .build/release/PanelGenerator after make app, or .build/debug/PanelGenerator.

After a rebuild, quit and reopen the app. A running copy keeps the old code. When a new menu item or palette entry is "missing", the window you are looking at was started before the build.

.panelgen files open from Finder, by dropping them on the Dock icon, or with open -a PanelGenerator file.panelgen. Fonts a plugin bundles in a res/fonts folder near the document (beside it, one level up, or one level into the parent) are loaded with it for this session, so labels set in them draw in their own face. Nothing is installed.

AreaWhat it does

Show at the top limits the list to one design language's sections (plus the generic primitives, symbols, text and stamps that every style uses); the choice is remembered. Layers (left, bottom)Every element in stacking order, with its role badge; select, hide, reorder.

Position fields: CX/CY are the centre, X/Y the top-left corner, W/H the size. They are in panel pixels with two decimals, or in millimetres with three after View ▸ Inspector in Millimetres. Type any value; fractions are kept.

Theme colours on elements

Each element's Theme row has two switches. Follows panel ink draws it in the current ink, so a label reads white on the dark face and black on the light one; for a stroke-only element (a line, a Paperface glyph) it is the line that follows. Follows panel background fills it with the panel colour, for a shape that masks what lies behind it; its line keeps its own colour. Paperface labels, titles and Paris glyphs follow the ink by default.

4 · Selecting

  • Click picks what a click means, not merely what is on top: a control before a label, a label or glyph before a filled shape, a filled shape before an outline. An outline-only shape (a Paperface cell, dome or diamond, a delineation box, a line) responds only near its line, so the jack inside a cell is simply the jack.
  • ⌥-click on the same spot steps through everything under the pointer.
  • ⇧-click or ⌘-click adds to or removes from the selection. Clicking a member of a group selects the whole group.
  • Drag on empty canvas draws a marquee. It takes everything it touches; with Edit ▸ Select ▸ Marquee Selects Enclosed Only it takes only what lies entirely inside.

Then set colours, line widths or fonts once in the inspector for all of them.

5 · Placing and arranging

Drag to move; arrow keys nudge by one pixel, ⇧+arrow by the snap step. The blue ↻ badge above a selection rotates it (⇧ snaps 15°). A dragged selection snaps once, by the element you grabbed, and everything moves by that same amount: internal distances never change, so grab a collection by one of its controls to put that control on the grid.

Grids and snapping

⇧⌘G toggles snapping. View ▸ Snap Step chooses the grid; the grid's points are drawn on the canvas while snapping is on. Placement and moving snap element centres; resize handles snap edges.

GridFor
Uniform steps (1 px … 1 HP)Plain pixel or HP raster.
Serge GridSerge's five rows (27.2, 48.2, 69.2, 90.2, 111.2 mm), 63 px columns, half rows and half columns between them. Serge Grid: Outer Half-Step adds one half row beyond the top and bottom rows (also used by the Paperface grid).
Custom Grid…An N × M division of the panel, with optional half and quarter positions.
Paperface GridSix rows with half rows, columns a whole number of HP apart, quarter positions and frame lines (§9). Opens automatically for a Paperface panel.

Half positions are offered on every row, including a half column on a main row: panels put lamps and small jacks there.

Smart guides

The edges and centres of every other visible element, and the panel's edges and centre lines, are guide lines (View ▸ Snap to Elements). A dragged selection's edges or centre, a resized edge, or a pen point within 6 screen pixels of one locks to it on that axis, ahead of the grid; the lines that caught show in magenta. On a Paperface panel the frame lines are guides too.

Something you can select but not move is a tracing template (imported as an underlay, drawn faint). Select it in the layer list and use Make Editable in the inspector to turn it into artwork. Emit warns about templates left in a document.

6 · Drawing shapes

The palette's shape sections hold boxes with per-corner radii (pills, capsules, notched brackets), ellipses, triangles, LCARS elbows and swirls, ring sectors, lines and symbols.

  • Pen (P on the canvas, or Draw ▸ Pen): click for corners, drag for curves, click the first point to close a filled shape, Return to finish an open line, Esc when done. Points snap to the grid and to smart guides; ⌥ places freely, ⇧ constrains to 45°.
  • Edit points: double-click a freeform shape, or Return with it selected. Drag points or handles, click the outline to add a point, ⌫ to delete, double-click a point to switch corner/smooth. It is a mode: click outside, or Esc, to leave it.
  • Edit ▸ Shape: Combine, Subtract Front from Back, Intersect and Exclude Overlap on selected shapes; Convert to Freeform Shape (boxes, ellipses, elbows, ring sectors, text); Outline Stroke turns a line into a filled outline.

7 · Text, labels and titles

Text

A text element's inspector has Content, Font (System or any installed or plugin-bundled family) and Style (its weights), Size, Tracking (extra space after each letter, in panel px, as nanoVG's letter spacing), and Bold for the system font. A face the document names but this Mac lacks is shown as "(missing)" and drawn in the system font. {ka}, {ru}, {te} … inside the text place a glyph inline.

Labels are artwork, not identifiers: what is drawn on the panel never reaches the code.

Labels that belong to a control

  • Label Selection… ⌘L makes a label for every selected control from its name, in one size; the new labels stay selected so Size resizes them together.
  • Name from Labels ⇧⌘L does the reverse: each control takes the nearest label within 8 mm as its identifier.
  • Label slots. A label that belongs to a control snaps to fixed slots measured from the control's centre, so labels under a jack and a knob line up whatever their sizes: 7.1 mm below or above, 2.8 mm further for each extra line, 5.5 mm to the side (Edit ▸ Label Slots… to change). A label dragged within 3 mm of a slot locks to it; elsewhere the grid applies.
  • Labels move with their control, when dragged and when nudged.
  • Attach Labels to Controls ties selected labels to their nearest control (or to the one control in the selection) and puts them on a slot; Snap Labels to Slots moves owned labels onto slots; Detach Labels unties them.

The module title

The title is a property of the panel (Panel ▸ Title), not an element you place. The design language draws it, centred, at a fixed height, so every module of a series has its title in the same place and nobody can nudge it out of line.

StyleTitle
PaperfaceFutura Medium capitals, cap height 2.0 mm, centre of the capitals 4.9 mm from the top (measured on the reference panels).
Kurkesmurfer, SergeSpaceTime's header: the Kurkesmurfer logo (5.2 mm tall), a 1.2 mm gap, the title in Fraunces SemiBold; logo and title centred together on a line 5.8 mm down.
LCARS, GenericA plain bold title centred 5.4 mm down.

It follows the theme's ink and appears on the canvas, in PNGs and in the exported SVG. Edit ▸ Make Module Title converts a title drawn by hand: select the label (and the logo next to it) and the text moves into the Title field.

8 · Components

Every element carries a component binding: a role, an identifier, and where its artwork comes from.

RoleBecomes
DecorationArtwork. Drawn into the panel SVG; never reaches the code.
Param / Input / Output / LightA Rack widget placed by the generated header, with an entry in the matching enum. Left out of the panel artwork, because Rack draws it on top.
CustomA widget of your own class.

Three things called "name"

WhereFieldWhat it is
Component sectionIdentifierQUANT → QUANT_PARAM in the generated enum
Text elementContentthe words drawn on the panel
Layer listlayer nameorganisational; feeds the identifier only when Identifier is empty

A fully labelled panel can have every control unnamed. Give each control its role, then its identifier: by hand, with Name from Labels ⇧⌘L, or with Adopt Identifiers…, which copies identifiers from another .panelgen or a module's .cpp by matching the label beside each control and its role (exact, then punctuation-insensitive, then by a shared leading run of at least three characters; loose matches are reported). That is what a redesign needs: the same controls in a new layout.

Identifiers are numbered per enum, not per panel. ParamId, InputId, OutputId and LightId are separate, so ANIMATE_PARAM and ANIMATE_INPUT are not a clash.

Rack type or your own artwork

  • Rack default (Artwork: Rack default, Rack type): Rack draws its own widget and takes the size from its own SVG, so the element on the canvas is a placement guide. The importer sizes imported widgets from Rack's ComponentLibrary when Rack is installed.
  • Custom (Custom struct): PanelGenerator draws the widget and writes its artwork to res/components/; the size you draw is the size in Rack. Knobs get a background and a rotating foreground (-bg/-fg), switches one file per position, ports one file.
  • Bind Primitives as Components gives every jack, knob, LED and button in the selection the role its kind implies.
  • Make Widget from Selection… turns a group of shapes into one composed custom widget (mark the parts that turn with Rotates with value).

Switches

Rack draws switches and buttons with app::SvgSwitch, which holds one SVG per position and picks one by parameter value; a button is the two-position case. A custom switch is exported once per position with the bat or cap moved; Position in the inspector shows each. Two things fail quietly:

  • momentary belongs only to a button. A three-way switch declared momentary snaps back to position 0.
  • The parameter range lives in the module. Three frames with the parameter still 0…1 reach only two of them. The generated code carries the call to place, e.g. configSwitch(GATE_MODE_PARAM, 0.f, 2.f, 0.f, "Mode", {"DC", "AC", "MOD"});

The positions you draw and the Rack type you bind must agree: a three-position group bound to VCVButton is a two-position momentary button in Rack. Check Panel for Issues warns.

Lights

An LED left on the default type is exported as the Rack light nearest what you drew: size Tiny, Small, Medium or Large (1.1 / 2.2 / 3.2 / 5.2 mm) and colour White, Red, Green, Blue or Yellow. The inspector shows it as Auto — MediumLight<YellowLight>; choose a type to fix it, and Auto to go back.

9 · Design languages

A design language is a house style: palette sections, swatches, presets, and the rules for the module title. The panel's Style picks one; the palette's Show lists its sections.

LanguageBrings
GenericPrimitives (jacks, knobs with or without a position ring, faders, LEDs, buttons, button groups, screws), the shape kit, symbols, text.
KurkesmurferDelineations and line work; the logo and wordmark stamps; the logo-and-title lockup.
SergeDelineations, notched brackets, knob-to-jack connectors; the Serge grid; the Kurkesmurfer title height for now.
LCARSElbows, swirls, ring sectors and the Okudagram swatches.
PaperfaceBelow.

Paperface

After the Serge Paperface 50 panels (Helios, Paris Flash, Tempo di Roma), scaled to 3U. All measurements are in Docs/PAPERFACE.md.

Palette

  • Hardware: banana jacks by signal colour (white CV, black audio, red trigger/logic, purple sync, grey special), the scalloped knob, lamps (Small = Paris 3.2 mm, Rack Medium; Large = Helios 5.2 mm, Rack Large; both yellow), a plain screw, and the bat toggle in 3-way (DC / AC / MOD) and 2-way versions. The toggle is a custom switch, PaperfaceToggle3 / PaperfaceToggle2, emitted once into the plugin's widget library with one drawing per position (up, end-on, down; value 0 is up).
  • Cells: cell, dome (attenuator), roof (pot), scale − +, label strip, funnel (input), diamond (output), arrow, connector. They stretch with their frame and keep their line weight; their default height is what each takes in one row.
  • Glyphs and text: the Helios glyphs (sine, saw, square; line grey) and the Paris glyphs (rise, fall, pulse, inverted pulse, attack/decay; ink, thin); a label and a section title.
  • Row units under Stamps ▸ Paperface: Pot, Attenuator, Output, Input, Trigger. A control with its row's decoration; dropped by the control onto the grid, labels attached.

Grid

Six object rows at 15.5 mm + 19.25 mm (measured), the half rows between them (where the cell lines fall, and where the third 1V/O jack or a START jack sits), columns a whole number of HP apart: 4 HP (20.32 mm) by default, so an n-column module is exactly n × 4 HP. View ▸ Snap Step ▸ Paperface Grid Settings… sets the first row, row pitch (mm, or px with a "px" suffix), row count, column HP and quarter positions per document.

Rows and sections

A row's decoration ends on the half row below its control (+9.625 mm): dome −7.0…+4.6 mm, scale +6.2…+9.625, roof −9.625…+4.6, label strip +6.0…+9.625, cell and diamond one full row. Three rows make one section (57.75 mm); copy a section with ⇧⌘D, 3 rows.

Frame

The artwork runs from the top line at 8.6 mm (first row − 6.9, the title strip above) to the bottom line at 121.375 mm (last row + half a row); a six-row panel has the double line between its sections at 63.625 / 66.35 mm. The lines are drawn green with the grid and are snap targets. Edit ▸ Fit Artwork to Frame snaps cell edges within 3 mm onto them (selection or whole panel), and the export report warns about cells outside the frame.

10 · Stamps

Fragments every panel of yours repeats: a logo, a wordmark, a jack pair with its labels, a Paperface row unit. Select, then Edit ▸ Add Selection to Palette… and name it; it appears under Stamps and drops like any palette item. A stamp carries shapes, sizes and colours, never identity: identifiers, groups and label bindings are stripped, so two panels never claim the same enum name. Each drop gets fresh ids and one group. A stamp built round one control is placed by that control, and its labels attach to it.

Stamps live in ~/Library/Application Support/PanelGenerator/Stamps as plain JSON, subfolders becoming palette sections. Edit ▸ Reveal Palette Stamps in Finder, then Reload Palette Stamps after changes. The ones in the repository's Stamps/ folder are installed on first launch and never overwrite yours.

11 · Importing

A panel SVG: File ▸ Import SVG… ⌘I

  • As a tracing template: drawn faint and locked, to draw over.
  • As editable artwork: paths, rectangles, circles and text become elements you own. A helper.py components layer brings its components (circles classified by fill, named through data-name, optional #WidgetClass).

Read: CSS in <style> and class attributes, <use> and <symbol>, clip paths, even-odd fills, hidden layers (components often live in one). Gradients become their average colour. Units come from the width attribute (a Rack panel's viewBox is in millimetres); a file without one is assumed pixels, and the report says so. Not read: embedded rasters, masks; multi-line text split over positioned <tspan>s arrives as one line. Text arrives as outlines.

A module's C++: File ▸ Import Module Code… ⌥⌘I

Reads a ModuleWidget constructor and rebuilds its components (positions, identifiers, widget types, switch positions) and the panel SVG it names, as artwork. It follows the source's quoted #includes into the plugin (a widgets/ folder beside src/ is found), evaluates constants, arrays, locals and loops, expands the plugin's own inline helpers that place controls or labels, reads labels from helper calls (with their size, font and letter spacing, loading the plugin's font files) and from constant string arrays, handles Vec.plus() / .minus(), sizes widgets from Rack's ComponentLibrary or the plugin's own SVGs, and takes the plugin slug from plugin.json. Anything it skipped is reported by line number.

Centered is not decoration. createParamCentered<T>(pos…) takes a centre; createParam<T>(pos…) a top-left corner. The offset is the widget's own size; when the importer cannot find it, the control is placed at the corner and listed.

Reports open in a window you can edit, copy and save; File ▸ Show Last Report reopens the last one. From the command line: --import-check reports what a file or folder would import as, --import-module builds a document from a module's C++ (§14).

12 · Exporting and emitting

From the editor

  • File ▸ Export SVG… ⌘E: the panel artwork, plus <name>-components.svg, a positions file in the shape helper.py expects.
  • File ▸ Export PNG… ⇧⌘E: the panel at 4×.
  • File ▸ Export Widget Code… ⌥⌘E: the C++ for this panel.
  • File ▸ Check Panel for Issues…: the warnings an export would give (default plugin slug, switches whose positions do not match their Rack type, artwork outside a Paperface frame, templates left in, pixel units…).

Emit: one command for a plugin

PanelGenerator --emit <document|folder> <plugin dir> [--theme dark|light|both]

A folder emits every panel in it. Artwork goes to res/ under the output directory; headers to src/ when it exists, else beside res/. Run it from the plugin root so nothing needs copying afterwards.

FileOwnerWhat
panels/*.panelgenyouthe source

The widget library belongs to the plugin, not the module. Two modules using the same jack must compile to one class loading one SVG, so emit builds the library from every panel of that plugin it finds: those in this run plus sibling documents with the same plugin slug. File names come from the widget class, never the instance. A panel still on the default MyPlugin slug groups with every other panel on that default: set it.

Commit the generated files with the document: a contributor without PanelGenerator can still build, and the header's diff records what moved. For MetaModule, the same sources compile through the SDK's rack adaptor; mirror res/ into the MetaModule assets as PNGs (the component SVGs carry width="…mm", which the scripts require).

PANELGEN ?= $(HOME)/Development/PanelGenerator/.build/release/PanelGenerator
PANELS   ?= panels

panels:            # regenerate artwork, widget library and headers
	$(PANELGEN) --emit $(PANELS) .

check-panels:      # fail when a panel was edited and nobody re-emitted
	@set -e; for p in $(PANELS)/*.panelgen; do \
		$(PANELGEN) --compare "$$p" "$$(basename "$$p" .panelgen)_panel.hpp" --no-labels; \
	done

Wiring the module

Delete the module's own enum. addComponents() places against the enum in <Module>_panel.hpp, whose order is the panel's reading order. A module that keeps its own enum ParamId compiles cleanly and binds every control to the wrong parameter.

#include "plugin.hpp"
#include "Muse_ND_panel.hpp"
using namespace Muse_NDPanel;          // ids unqualified, as Rack code expects

struct MuseND : Module {
    MuseND() {
        config(PARAMS_LEN, INPUTS_LEN, OUTPUTS_LEN, LIGHTS_LEN);
        configSwitch(QUANT_PARAM, 0.f, 2.f, 0.f, "Quant", {"Free", "JI", "Octave"});
    }
    void process(const ProcessArgs& args) override { /* yours */ }
};

struct MuseNDWidget : ModuleWidget {
    MuseNDWidget(MuseND* module) {
        setModule(module);
        addComponents(this, module);
    }
};

13 · Comparing

A panel exists in three forms at once: the document, the exported artwork and the module's C++, and nothing stops them drifting apart. --compare checks that two of them agree.

PanelGenerator --compare <a> <b> [--tolerance <mm>] [--no-labels]
make compare A=<file> B=<file> [TOL=<mm>] [NOLABELS=1]

What each side can be

Each side is reduced to its components (identifier, role, widget type, centre in mm) and, where the file has them, its labels. Any two can be paired.

FileWhat is readLabels
.panelgencomponents and text labels in the documentyes
.cpp / .hppthe create… calls of a generated _panel.hpp or a hand-written module, plus label helper callshand-written: yes; generated: no
.svgthe components layer only (e.g. NegativeSlew-components.svg), not the artworkno

Useful pairs: document ↔ generated header (is the code I build current?), document ↔ hand-written module (does my redraw match the module?), document ↔ components SVG, and two documents (what moved between versions?).

Use --no-labels whenever one side is generated code or an SVG. PanelGenerator draws labels into the artwork, not into the code, so otherwise every label is reported missing and the comparison fails on labels alone.

Reading the report

First line under the header: both files carry a provenance digest (// PanelGenerator-Source: Muse ND · 25 components · digest 4717c5f5), computed over identifier, role, widget type and position, not colours. If it says DIFFERENT PANELS, one file is stale or from another panel, and the differences below say nothing about a fault: re-emit and compare again.

DifferenceMeans
movedsame identifier, more than the tolerance apart (default 0.01 mm; the three forms round differently, so zero would report everything)
rolee.g. input on one side, output on the other: the cable that will not connect
widgeta different Rack type or custom class (e.g. a header from before a lamp's colour changed)
renamedsame position, different identifier
missing / addedon one side only

Anything a reader could not resolve is listed as a note, not a difference: skipped is not absent.

Ends withExitMeans
COMPARE OK — the two agree.0no differences
COMPARE FAILED — N differences.1the comparison ran; read the list above it
COMPARE FAILED: <reason>2a file could not be read (missing, unknown type, does not parse)

For a change to PanelGenerator itself, the round trip is the strongest test: make selftest, then emit a document and compare it with its own generated header.

15 · What bites, and limits

What bites

  • Millimetres, not pixels. Read a millimetre position as pixels and every component lands at a third of its place.
  • Centered is not decoration: centre versus top-left corner.
  • The generated enum owns the ids: reading order, not writing order; delete the module's own enum.
  • Identifiers are numbered per enum.
  • One SVG per switch position, and the range lives in the module.
  • File names come from the class, never the instance.
  • One document per module. Two documents claiming one module slug overwrite each other; emit refuses.
  • Restart the app after a rebuild.
  • Compare against code with --no-labels.

What the tool cannot do

  • No gradients: fills are solid; an imported gradient becomes its average colour.
  • No embedded rasters or masks on import.
  • Knob rotation is not exported: Rack sweeps knobs itself, so artwork points up.
  • Text must stay outlines for Rack (nanosvg drops <text>); live text is only for other editors.
  • A light/dark pair comes from one document with a light variant, not from two separate files.
